Incident Briefing

Asterion Research Systems recently detected unusual outbound network activity originating from its internal research network. Monitoring systems recorded repeated DNS queries to an unfamiliar external domain. Around the same time, an internal research repository registered an unexpected access event.

The security team suspects a compromised workstation transferred confidential research material outside the organization. The endpoint investigation was interrupted before the workstation could be isolated, but several pieces of evidence were preserved.

Incident reference: ARS-2026-041  |  All timestamps in the evidence are IST (UTC+05:30).

Objectives

  1. Identify the workstation responsible for the suspicious activity.
  2. Determine which network mechanism was abused.
  3. Reconstruct the information transferred outside the organization.
  4. Submit both recovered flags.

Progressive Hints

H1

An incident timeline is often more useful than examining every packet individually. Start by understanding when the unusual activity occurred.

H2

DNS traffic is not limited to ordinary hostname lookups. Pay attention to the structure of the requested names.

H3

A suspicious source IP is only an address. Can you establish which device was using it at the time?

H4

DNS query names can contain more information than the destination domain itself. Examine the labels carefully.

H5

The captured data appears to have been divided into multiple pieces. Reconstruct the original sequence before attempting to decode it.
